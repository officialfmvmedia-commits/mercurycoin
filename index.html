01 — THE LAST LIGHT

On April 30, 2015, a spacecraft took one last picture of Mercury. Eight hours later it became a crater.

MESSENGER was the first spacecraft ever to orbit the innermost planet. For four years it circled a world scorched by a Sun that looks up to three times wider than it does from Earth, and it sent home more than 270,000 images.

Then it ran out of fuel. At 11:07:43 UTC, 28.9 km above the floor of Jokai crater, its narrow-angle camera took a final frame. At 19:26 UTC it struck the surface at 3.9 km/s.

That last frame is archived by NASA's Planetary Data System, byte for byte. $MERCURY is the first coin whose mint key is seeded from it. Not a mascot. A real photograph, hashed and folded into the keypair that creates the token.

  1. Download CN1072716050M_IF_5.IMG from the PDS Imaging Node (link above). It is 1,058,816 bytes.
  2. sha256 it. You get cec65107…ab7142, the Mercury hash.
  3. sha256 the revealed entropy bytes. It must equal the commitment published before launch.
  4. Run HKDF-SHA256(ikm = entropy ‖ mercury_hash, salt = "MERCURY-MINT-v1", info = "CN1072716050M_IF_52015-04-30T11:07:43.368128", L = 32).

06 — FAQ

Questions from Earth.

Is the image real?

Yes. It is MESSENGER's final image, MDIS narrow-angle frame EN1072716050M (MESSENGER image ID 8422953, released by NASA as PIA19448). The hashed file is the calibrated version, CN1072716050M_IF_5.IMG, on the PDS volume MSGRMDS_2001. Its MD5 matches the archive's own MD5.TAB.

Why can't you pick a nicer address?

The image was fixed in 2015. Our entropy is locked by a hash we publish before launch. Once both are committed there is exactly one possible address, and anyone can recompute it after the reveal.

Can someone steal the key from the image?

No. The image is public; the 32 bytes of entropy are not. Without them, finding the seed means searching 2256 possibilities. The entropy is only revealed after the mint exists and its authorities are revoked, so the key controls nothing by then.

What happens if NASA re-processes the file?

Archived PDS products are stable, but a future re-release would have different bytes. We mirror the exact file on this site, and the SHA-256 above is the reference.

Why now?

ESA and JAXA's BepiColombo is scheduled to enter orbit around Mercury on 21 November 2026, the first spacecraft there since MESSENGER. $MERCURY is minted from the last light the first one sent home.

No. It uses public-domain data. $MERCURY is a memecoin with no intrinsic value or expectation of return.
